# Interview question outlines

Adapt these outlines to the case, approved CDCR procedures, and applicable pre/post-offer restrictions. They are prompts for complete fact gathering, not scripts for accusatory questioning.

## Pre-Investigatory Interview

- What information has changed since the PHS was submitted?
- Are all residences, employers, schools, applications, relationships, legal matters, and driving events complete?
- Are there gaps or overlapping dates that need clarification?
- Which records or contacts may use another name, spelling, address, telephone number, or identifier?
- Is any listed source unavailable, and what alternate source can verify the same facts?
- Does any answer need correction before investigative inquiries begin?

## Employer contact

- Confirm dates, role, duties, and eligibility for rehire.
- Confirm attendance, reliability, policy compliance, safety, judgment, interpersonal conduct, and communication when lawfully releasable.
- Identify documented discipline, commendations, separation circumstances, and discrepancies with the candidate's account.
- Ask for the appropriate records custodian and any secondary supervisor or coworker source.

## Personal or secondary reference

- Establish relationship, duration, frequency, and recency of contact.
- Obtain behavior-based examples relevant to the POST dimensions.
- Clarify whether knowledge is firsthand or hearsay.
- Ask about contradictions directly but neutrally.
- Request additional people with meaningful firsthand knowledge.

## Neighbor contact

- Establish residency overlap and opportunity to observe.
- Ask about ordinary interactions, reliability, conflict, disturbances, safety-related conduct, and community behavior.
- Separate firsthand observation from reputation or rumor.

## Discrepancy interview

- Provide the written issue description according to approved procedure.
- Ask the candidate to explain the difference fully in their own words.
- Establish dates, sequence, participants, records, and potential corroborating sources.
- Clarify whether the original answer was mistaken, incomplete, omitted, or based on different terminology.
- Ask what evidence could confirm the explanation.
- Do not announce or promise an outcome.
