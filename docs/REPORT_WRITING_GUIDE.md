# Neutral investigative writing guide

## Evidence pattern

Use a repeatable structure:

1. **Source and date:** identify who or what supplied the information.
2. **Material fact:** state the relevant information without embellishment.
3. **Candidate account:** record the candidate's explanation when applicable.
4. **Corroboration:** identify confirming or conflicting records and contacts.
5. **Disposition:** explain whether the matter was resolved, remains unresolved, or requires review.

## Preferred language

- “The employment record lists…”
- “The candidate stated…”
- “The former supervisor reported…”
- “The accounts differ regarding…”
- “No independent record was located to corroborate…”
- “The investigator documented three attempts on…”

Avoid unsupported labels such as “dishonest,” “obviously,” “bad attitude,” or “untrustworthy.” Describe the conduct and evidence so the reviewing authority can apply the standard.

## Source discipline

- Cite the registered source identifier for every material finding.
- Identify hearsay and the original speaker when known.
- Do not convert silence or nonresponse into a negative finding.
- Preserve favorable, mitigating, and contradictory information along with adverse information.
- Limit sensitive content to what is relevant and lawfully reportable.

## Final review

- Read the report as the hiring authority, psychologist, candidate-access reviewer, auditor, and litigation reviewer might read it.
- Confirm names, dates, quotations, legal authorities, and source identifiers.
- Remove duplicated narrative and irrelevant personal information.
- Confirm the approved CDCR template and current authority before submission.
